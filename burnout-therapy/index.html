Burnout therapy · Virtual across Canada

Burnout therapy that helps you rest again

If you are running on empty — tired no matter how much you sleep, numb at work, snapping at the people you love — burnout is not a personal failure. It is a sign that you have been carrying too much for too long.

You might be here because…

Burnout can build slowly, then arrive all at once.

You might be here because work never really ends. The emails, the extra shifts, the expectations — at the office, at home, or in caring for others — keep coming, and you keep saying yes. You wake up tired, drag yourself through the day, and collapse at night, only to do it again tomorrow. The things that used to give you energy now feel like one more demand.

Or burnout might show up as detachment. You feel numb or cynical about work you once cared about, you struggle to focus, and small tasks feel enormous. You might feel guilty for resting, or afraid that slowing down means falling behind. You might wonder why everyone else seems to cope while you feel so depleted. Burnout is not laziness, and it is not a lack of resilience. It often grows where people give a great deal, for a long time, without enough rest, support, or room to say no.

Root-cause healing for burnout

We work on where the pain started, not just how to cope with it.

Rest, boundaries, and better sleep can help, and we will find practical ways to protect your energy. But burnout often has deeper roots: beliefs that your worth depends on how much you produce, a habit of putting everyone else first, a workplace or family role where you were the one who always held things together, or a season when there was simply no space to stop.

Root-cause healing means we look honestly at those patterns — where they came from, what they cost you, and what they once protected you from. We explore the guilt that comes with resting, the fear of disappointing others, and the voice that says you must keep going no matter what. Together, we work toward a steadier way of living: one where rest is not something you earn after collapse, where boundaries are not a betrayal, and where your energy belongs to you again, not only to everyone else.

What therapy for burnout looks like

Practical, gentle, and honest about your real life.

Sessions are 50 minutes, by secure video, from a private place that feels comfortable to you. We talk about your week, your work, your relationships, and what rest actually looks like for you — not an ideal version, but what is possible in your real life. Yeukai draws on approaches she is trained in, including Acceptance and Commitment Therapy, Internal Family Systems, and Somatic Therapy, choosing what fits your story.

Some sessions focus on the body: noticing exhaustion, tension, and the signals you have been pushing past, and learning to listen to them sooner. Others focus on boundaries — how to say no, how to ask for help, how to have hard conversations at work or at home without carrying all the guilt. We may look at the values underneath the exhaustion: what matters to you, what you want your days to hold, and what a sustainable life could look like. There is no pressure to quit your job or to fix everything at once. Change here is steady and realistic, built around the life you actually live.

If burnout is tangled up with anxiety, low mood, or low self-esteem, we can work on those threads too. You can also read about anxiety therapy, depression therapy, or self-esteem therapy.

Is burnout the same as stress?

They are related, but burnout is usually longer and deeper. Stress often eases when the pressure lifts; burnout can linger as exhaustion, detachment, and a sense that you have nothing left to give, even after rest.

Do I need to quit my job to recover from burnout?

Not necessarily, and therapy will not push you to. We work on what is possible in your real life — rest, boundaries, support, and how you relate to work — and you decide what changes, if any, to make.

What if I feel guilty about resting?

That guilt is very common, and it is part of what we work on. Many people learned that rest has to be earned, or that slowing down lets others down. Therapy helps you explore where that belief came from and practise rest without the guilt.

How long does burnout therapy take?

It depends on your story, your work, and your goals. Some people feel steadier in a few sessions; others work together longer, especially when burnout is tied to long-standing patterns. We review progress together, and you decide the pace.

Can I do therapy from home?

Yes. All sessions are virtual, by secure video, across Canada. You choose a private, comfortable place, and you do not need to travel or sit in a waiting room.
