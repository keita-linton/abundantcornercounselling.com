Insurance & benefits · Receipts for every paid session

Using your benefits for therapy

Many people in Canada can use workplace or private health benefits to help cover therapy. This page explains how receipts work, what to check in your own plan, and what this practice does — and does not — do with billing.

How it works here

Simple: you pay at session time, and you get a receipt.

After every paid session, you receive a receipt you can submit to your benefits plan. The receipt shows the session, the fee, and Yeukai Tsikira's credentials: Registered Psychotherapist (Qualifying), CRPO registration 18718, and Canadian Certified Counsellor (CCC), CCPA member 11246478. That is the information most plans ask for when they review a claim.

Payment is due at session time, by credit card or e-transfer via Jane. There is no direct billing. That means the practice does not bill your insurer directly, and you do not wait for an insurer to approve a session before you book. You pay for the session, you receive your receipt, and you submit it to your plan yourself, in whatever way your plan asks — usually online, by app, or by mail.

Fees are $120 for individual therapy (50 minutes), $180 for family therapy (75 minutes), and $260 for EMDR (90 minutes). A limited $50-per-session promotion is available for ongoing individual therapy only, while spots last. Your receipt reflects the fee you actually paid for that session.

Which plans typically cover this kind of therapy?

Coverage varies by plan — always check yours.

Many Canadian group benefits plans include coverage for registered psychotherapist or Canadian Certified Counsellor services, often under a mental health, counselling, or paramedical benefit. As examples only, plans from major Canadian group insurers such as Sun Life, Manulife, Canada Life, GreenShield, and Alberta Blue Cross commonly include this kind of coverage — but only in some plans, and only up to the limits that plan sets. Naming them here is not a promise that your plan covers therapy, and it is not a promise about how much it will pay.

Coverage varies by plan, so the only way to know what yours covers is to check it directly. Some plans cover a set amount per session, some cover a yearly maximum, some cover a percentage of the fee, and some do not include psychotherapy or counselling at all. Your plan may also use different words — for example, it may list a registered psychotherapist, a Canadian Certified Counsellor, a psychotherapist, or a counsellor — and it may ask for a specific credential or registration number. The receipts from this practice include Yeukai's CRPO registration number and CCC membership number, so you can share those details when you ask.

How to check your own coverage

Five questions to ask your insurer or benefits administrator.

  1. Does my plan cover Registered Psychotherapist (Qualifying) services, or Canadian Certified Counsellor (CCC) services? Ask about both, and ask whether “Qualifying” matters to your plan.
  2. How much is covered per session, and what is the yearly maximum? Ask whether there is a per-visit limit, a percentage, or a combined mental health maximum.
  3. Do I need a referral or a doctor's note? Most plans do not, but some do — and it is better to know before your first paid session.
  4. What do I need to submit? Usually a receipt, and sometimes a claim form. Ask whether receipts must show a registration number.
  5. Does coverage apply to virtual sessions? Ask whether secure video sessions count the same as in-person sessions under your plan.

You can usually find these answers in your benefits booklet, on your insurer's website or app, or by calling the number on your benefits card. If you are unsure what to ask, bring your questions to your free 15-minute consultation — Yeukai can help you think through what to check, though she cannot check your plan for you or speak to your insurer on your behalf.

Do you bill my insurance directly?

No. There is no direct billing. You pay at session time by credit card or e-transfer via Jane, and you receive a receipt after every paid session to submit to your plan yourself.

Will my plan cover sessions with a Registered Psychotherapist (Qualifying)?

It depends on your plan. Many Canadian group plans include registered psychotherapist or CCC coverage, but coverage varies by plan. Check your benefits booklet or ask your insurer whether Registered Psychotherapist (Qualifying) and Canadian Certified Counsellor services are covered, and whether “Qualifying” matters.

What is on the receipt?

Your receipt shows the session and the fee you paid, along with Yeukai Tsikira's credentials: Registered Psychotherapist (Qualifying), CRPO registration 18718, and Canadian Certified Counsellor (CCC), CCPA member 11246478.

Do you know if Sun Life, Manulife, or Canada Life will cover me?

No — and no practice can promise that. Plans from insurers such as Sun Life, Manulife, Canada Life, GreenShield, and Alberta Blue Cross commonly include this kind of coverage in some plans, but coverage varies by plan. Only your insurer can confirm what your plan covers.

Is the free consultation covered by insurance?

The free 15-minute consultation costs nothing, so there is nothing to claim. Receipts are provided for paid sessions only.

Do I need a referral for insurance?

You do not need a referral to book therapy here. Some benefits plans ask for a referral or a doctor's note before they pay a claim, so check your own plan before your first paid session if you plan to claim.
