Self-esteem therapy · Virtual across Canada

Self-esteem therapy that helps you trust yourself

If you are always second-guessing yourself, apologising for taking up space, or feeling like you are not enough — you are not the problem. The voice that tells you that is something you learned, and it can be unlearned.

You might be here because…

Low self-esteem can be loud, or it can be very quiet.

You might be here because you doubt yourself constantly. You replay what you said, worry about what people think, and assume the worst about how you came across. You might hold back from applying for a job, speaking up in a meeting, or asking for what you need — not because you have nothing to say, but because a voice inside says you are not good enough, smart enough, or worthy enough.

Or low self-esteem might show up in relationships: saying yes when you mean no, staying in situations that hurt you, or working hard to be liked while feeling unseen. You might compare yourself to everyone else and always come up short, or feel like an impostor waiting to be found out — even when you have done good work. You might be kind to everyone except yourself. None of that means you are flawed. It usually means you learned, somewhere along the way, that you had to earn your worth.

Root-cause healing for self-esteem

We work on where the pain started, not just how to cope with it.

Affirmations and confidence tips can help for a moment, and we may find practical ways to speak up and set boundaries. But low self-esteem often has roots: messages from childhood, criticism or comparison, experiences of being dismissed or hurt, or seasons when love and approval felt conditional. When those roots stay untouched, the doubt tends to return, no matter how much you achieve.

Root-cause healing means we gently explore where the belief that you are not enough came from — and whose voice it really is. We notice the parts of you that criticise, shrink, or people-please, and we learn to understand what they were trying to protect. Slowly, many people find a steadier relationship with themselves: less second-guessing, more trust in their own judgement, and a kinder voice inside. This is not about becoming arrogant or pretending to be confident. It is about knowing your worth is not something you have to prove.

What therapy for self-esteem looks like

Warm, honest, and built around your real life.

Sessions are 50 minutes, by secure video, from a private place that feels comfortable to you. We talk about your week, your relationships, and the moments when self-doubt shows up most. Yeukai draws on approaches she is trained in, including Internal Family Systems, Acceptance and Commitment Therapy, and Somatic Therapy, choosing what fits your story.

Some sessions focus on the inner critic — learning to notice it, to question it, and to respond with more compassion. Others focus on real-life practice: saying no, asking for what you need, taking up space in a conversation, or letting yourself be seen without apologising for it. We may explore the body too — how shame or doubt feels physically, and what helps you feel grounded and steady. You will not be told to simply love yourself, and you will not be given a list of affirmations to repeat. Change here is honest and gradual, built from small moments of trusting yourself again.

If low self-esteem is tangled up with anxiety, low mood, burnout, or past trauma, we can work on those threads too. You can also read about anxiety therapy, depression therapy, burnout therapy, or trauma and PTSD therapy.

Is low self-esteem something therapy can help with?

Yes. Low self-esteem is a common reason people come to therapy. We explore where the self-doubt came from, how it shows up in your daily life, and how to build a steadier, kinder relationship with yourself.

Will therapy just tell me to repeat affirmations?

No. Affirmations alone rarely reach the roots of low self-esteem. Therapy looks at the experiences and messages that shaped how you see yourself, and helps you practise trusting yourself in real situations.

What if I do not know why I feel this way?

That is completely fine. Many people come to therapy without a clear reason for their self-doubt. We explore your story together, gently, and understanding often grows as we talk.

How long does self-esteem therapy take?

It depends on your story and your goals. Some people notice a shift in a few sessions; others work together longer, especially when self-doubt is tied to earlier experiences. We review progress together, and you decide the pace.

Can I do therapy from home?

Yes. All sessions are virtual, by secure video, across Canada. You choose a private, comfortable place, and you do not need to travel or sit in a waiting room.
