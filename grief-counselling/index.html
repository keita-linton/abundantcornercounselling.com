Grief counselling · Virtual across Canada

Grief counselling that makes room for your loss

Grief does not follow a timetable, and it does not always look the way other people expect. Here, your loss — and the person, dream, or life you lost — is given time, respect, and space.

You might be here because…

Grief can arrive after many kinds of loss.

You might be here because someone you love has died, and the world kept moving while yours stopped. The mornings feel strange, familiar places ache, and waves of sadness arrive without warning — in the grocery store, in the car, in the middle of an ordinary day. You might feel numb one moment and overwhelmed the next, or guilty for laughing, resting, or beginning to feel a little steadier.

Or your grief might be for something else: the end of a relationship, a job or home you loved, a future you had imagined, a change in your health, or a loss that others do not recognise as grief at all. You might feel pressure to move on, to be strong, or to grieve in a certain way. There is no right way to grieve, and there is no deadline. If your loss still lives in you, it deserves a place to be spoken about honestly.

Root-cause healing for grief

We work on where the pain started, not just how to cope with it.

Coping can help you get through the day — a routine, a walk, a person who listens — and we will find what steadies you. But grief is not only about the present moment. It is tied to the relationship, the story, and the meaning of what was lost, and to earlier losses that a new loss can stir up again.

Root-cause healing in grief means we gently explore what this loss means to you: what you loved, what you miss, what was left unsaid, and what has changed in your life and in how you see yourself. We make room for anger, relief, guilt, love, and confusion — sometimes all in the same session — without judging any of it. The aim is not to get over your loss or to leave it behind. It is to help you carry it differently, so that grief becomes part of your story rather than the whole of it, and so that life can slowly hold both sorrow and meaning again.

What grief counselling looks like

Unhurried, honest, and led by you.

Sessions are 50 minutes, by secure video, from a private place that feels comfortable to you. You might talk about the person or the life you lost, share memories, or sit quietly with feelings that are hard to name. Yeukai draws on approaches she is trained in, including Acceptance and Commitment Therapy, Internal Family Systems, and Somatic Therapy, choosing what fits your grief rather than fitting your grief into a set of stages.

There is no pressure to find a silver lining, and no expectation that you will feel better by a certain date. Some sessions focus on the practical side of loss — how to get through anniversaries, holidays, or hard conversations with family. Others focus on the bond that continues: how to remember, honour, and stay connected to what mattered, while still living your own life. Family members grieve differently, and if that is part of your story, family therapy may also be an option to talk about together.

If grief is tangled up with anxiety, low mood, or trauma, we can work on those threads too. You can also read about depression therapy or trauma and PTSD therapy.

Is it too soon — or too late — to get grief counselling?

Neither. Some people come in the first weeks after a loss; others come months or years later, when grief resurfaces. Whenever you are ready to talk, you are welcome here.

Do I have to talk about the person I lost?

Only as much as you wish, and only when you are ready. You lead the conversation. Some people share many memories; others need time before they can speak about their loss at all.

What if my loss is not a death?

Grief counselling is for many kinds of loss — a relationship, a job, a home, your health, or a future you had hoped for. If it mattered to you and it hurts, it counts.

How long does grief counselling take?

It depends on your loss, your story, and your goals. Some people come for a short season of support; others work together longer. We review how things are going, and you decide the pace.

Can my family come too?

Yes. Family therapy is available for 75 minutes at $180. Families often grieve differently, and joint sessions can help everyone feel heard. We can talk about whether individual, family, or a mix fits best.
