What EMDR is

EMDR (Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing) is a therapy that can help your brain process painful memories so they may feel less present and less distressing over time. It is often used for trauma and PTSD. Outcomes vary from person to person, and EMDR is not the right fit for everyone, which is part of what the free consultation is for.

Survival mode

Surviving Is Not the Same as Living

Survival mode is not a character flaw. It is what your mind and body do when life has asked too much for too long. You keep functioning, because you have to. You smile at work, you show up for everyone, and underneath you are running on empty, sleeping badly, snapping at people you love, or feeling numb in moments that should feel like something.

Over time, that state starts to cost you. Your body stays braced even when nothing is wrong. Small things feel bigger than they are. Rest stops working the way it used to, because the system never really stands down. Relationships feel it too, because there is only so much of you to go around.

One early sign that you are ready for more than coping is that getting through the day no longer feels like enough. Another is noticing the pattern itself, that the same reactions keep showing up even when you try hard to do things differently. In therapy, we look at where those patterns began and what they were protecting you from, and we work at that root, at your pace. You do not have to have the right words to start.

EMDR · Trauma

What EMDR Actually Feels Like

Most people imagine EMDR is intense from the first minute. In practice, it starts slowly. Before any reprocessing, we spend time on your history, what you want to work on, and building steadiness, the tools that help you feel grounded if difficult feelings come up. We only move forward when that foundation feels solid enough.

In a reprocessing session, you bring a memory to mind briefly while following a back-and-forth movement, often eye movements, guided by your therapist. You do not have to describe every detail out loud. Your mind does the work in short sets, with pauses to check in. Sessions here are 90 minutes, held by secure video, and you can pause or stop at any point.

People describe the experience in different ways, and outcomes vary. For many, a memory that once felt present and sharp can, over time, feel more distant and less distressing. EMDR is not right for everyone, and it is not a quick fix. The free consultation is where we talk honestly about whether it fits what you are carrying.

Grief · Transitions

When Life Changes and You Don’t Recognise Yourself

Grief is not only about death. People grieve a marriage that ended, a career that changed, a move, a diagnosis, the version of life they expected to be living by now. Often the hardest part is not the event itself. It is the in-between, where the old life is gone and the new one has not taken shape yet, and you do not quite recognise yourself.

That in-between can look like going through the motions, feeling flat or irritable, or wondering why you cannot just be grateful and move on. There is nothing wrong with you for finding it hard. A transition asks you to let go of an identity, not just a circumstance, and that takes time.

Therapy gives that season a place to be spoken out loud, without rushing you toward a silver lining. We make sense of what was lost, what remains, and what you want the next chapter to actually be, so the change becomes something you move through, not something you carry alone.

What is EMDR?

EMDR stands for Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing.

It is a well-established, evidence-based approach for trauma and PTSD. In EMDR, you focus briefly on a distressing memory while following bilateral stimulation — usually eye movements, or taps — guided by your therapist. Research suggests this helps the brain reprocess the memory, so it stops feeling like a present danger. EMDR does not require you to retell every detail, and it does not promise a cure. It is steady, structured work, done at your pace, with grounding skills built first.

What an EMDR session looks like

EMDR sessions are 90 minutes and cost $260.

We begin with history and preparation — what you have been through, what still triggers you, and grounding skills you can use between sessions. When you feel ready, we begin reprocessing one memory at a time. After each set, we pause, notice what came up, then continue. Sessions end with grounding and a check-in, and you can pause or stop at any time.

Qu'est-ce que l'EMDR?

Une approche bien établie et fondée sur des données probantes pour le trauma et le TSPT.

EMDR signifie « désensibilisation et retraitement par les mouvements oculaires ». C'est une forme de psychothérapie qui aide le cerveau à retraiter des souvenirs difficiles qui n'ont pas été complètement intégrés. Pendant une séance d'EMDR, vous vous concentrez brièvement sur un souvenir, une image ou une émotion, tout en suivant une stimulation bilatérale — par exemple des mouvements oculaires guidés ou des tapotements alternés.

La recherche suggère que, pour beaucoup de personnes, l'EMDR peut réduire l'intensité des souvenirs traumatiques, les cauchemars, les flashbacks et l'hypervigilance. Vous n'avez pas besoin de raconter votre histoire en détail pour que l'EMDR fonctionne. Et vous n'avez pas besoin de revivre l'événement de la même façon qu'il s'est produit.
